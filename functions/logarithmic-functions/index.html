3.4 Logarithmic Functions

Logarithmic functions arise naturally when we wish to determine an unknown power. They are widely used in engineering, science, economics and data analysis, particularly when modelling quantities that vary over many orders of magnitude.

Definition

The logarithmic function is written in the form

\[ y=\log _a(x) \]

where:

  • \(a\gt 0\) is the base,

  • \(a\neq 1\),

  • \(x\gt 0\).

The logarithm answers the question:

“To what power must the base \(a\) be raised to obtain \(x\)?”

In other words,

\[ y=\log _a(x) \]

is equivalent to

\[ a^y=x \]

3.4.2 Common and Natural Logarithms

Although logarithms can have many different bases, two are used most frequently.

Definition

The two most common logarithms are:

  • The common logarithm

    \[ \log _{10}(x), \]

    usually written simply as

    \[ \log (x) \]

  • The natural logarithm

    \[ \log _e(x), \]

    written as

    \[ \ln (x), \]

    where

    \[ e\approx 2.71828 \]

In engineering and science, the natural logarithm is used most frequently.

Worked Example

Evaluate the following using a calculator:

\[ \log (1000) \]

Since

\[ 10^3=1000 \]

we have

\[ \log (1000)=3 \]

3.4.3 The Graph of a Logarithmic Function

The graph of a logarithmic function has a characteristic shape.

Definition

For the logarithmic function

\[ y=\ln (x) \quad \text{and} \quad y=\log _{10}(x) \]

the following properties hold:

  • The domain is \(x\gt 0\).

  • The graph passes through \((1,0)\).

  • There is no \(y\)-intercept.

  • The \(y\)-axis is a vertical asymptote.

  • The function increases as \(x\) increases.

More generally, logarithmic functions often appear in the form

\[ y=A\ln (x)+B, \]

where \(A\) and \(B\) are constants.

Definition

For

\[ y=A\ln (x)+B \]

  • Increasing \(|A|\) stretches the graph vertically.

  • If \(A\lt 0\), the graph is reflected in the \(x\)-axis.

  • The constant \(B\) shifts the graph vertically.

3.4.5 Logarithms and Exponentials

Logarithmic functions and exponential functions are inverse functions.

Definition

The natural logarithmic and exponential functions undo each other.

\[ \ln (\operatorname {e}^x)=x \]

and

\[ \operatorname {e}^{\ln (x)}=x \]
Worked Example

Simplify \(\ln (\operatorname {e}^7)\).

Since logarithms and exponentials are inverse functions,

\[ \ln (\operatorname {e}^7)=7 \]
Worked Example

Simplify \(\operatorname {e}^{\ln (15)}\).

Since logarithms and exponentials are inverse functions,

\[ \operatorname {e}^{\ln (15)}=15 \]

3.4.6 Applications of Logarithms

Engineering Application

Logarithms are frequently used to solve equations where the unknown quantity appears as a power. For example,

\[ 2^x=15 \]

Taking logarithms gives

\[ x=\log _2(15) \]

Without logarithms, equations of this form are often difficult to solve.

Engineering Application

Logarithmic scales are commonly used to measure quantities that vary over a very large range.

Logarithmic scales are used when values span a very large range because they compress large numbers while still showing smaller values clearly. This allows equal ratios (for example, 10 to 100 and 100 to 1000) to occupy equal distances on the scale, making patterns and trends easier to see. Examples include:

  • Sound intensity (decibels),

  • Earthquake magnitude,

  • Signal processing,

  • Information theory.

2.2 Simplification and Substitution

In the previous section we introduced variables, terms, coefficients and the basic techniques of algebra. In this section we combine those skills to simplify larger expressions, factorise expressions and evaluate formulae by substitution. These are essential skills throughout engineering mathematics.

  • Expanding brackets.

2.2.2 Negative Brackets

Negative brackets are a common source of errors. A minus sign immediately before a bracket means that every term in the bracket is multiplied by \(-1\).

Worked Example

Simplify

\[ -(x+5) \]

Multiplying every term by \(-1\) gives

\[ -x-5 \]
Worked Example

Simplify

\[ 4x-(2x-3) \]

Remove the bracket carefully:

\[ 4x-2x+3 \]

Now collect like terms and simplify:

\[ 2x+3 \]

Therefore,

\[ 4x-(2x-3)=2x+3 \]
Worked Example

Simplify

\[ 5a-(3a+7)+2 \]

2.2.3 Factorisation

Expanding brackets and factorisation are opposite processes. For example,

\[ 3x(x+2)=3x^2+6x \]

Factorisation starts with the expanded form and works backwards.

Definition
Factorisation is the process of identifying common factors and writing them outside a bracket.
Worked Example

Factorise

\[ 3x+6 \]

Both terms contain a factor of \(3\). Removing the common factor gives

\[ 3(x+2) \]
Worked Example

Factorise

\[ 3x+2xy \]

Both terms contain \(x\). Removing \(x\) gives

\[ x(3+2y) \]
Worked Example

Factorise

\[ 12x^2-8xy^2 \]

The largest common factor is \(4x\). Removing \(4x\) gives

\[ 12x^2-8xy^2 = 4x(3x-2y^2) \]

2.2.4 Simple Algebraic Fractions

Before studying algebraic fractions in detail, it is useful to see that many algebraic fractions can be simplified exactly like numerical fractions.

Worked Example

Simplify

\[ \frac{6x}{3} \]

Dividing the numerator and denominator by \(3\) gives

\[ 2x \]
Worked Example

Simplify

\[ \frac{12x^2}{4x} \]

Simplify the numerical part:

\[ \frac{12}{4}=3 \]

Simplify the algebraic part (using rules of indices):

\[ \frac{x^2}{x}=x \]

Therefore,

\[ \frac{12x^2}{4x}=3x \]

Notice that both numbers and variables can be cancelled provided they appear as factors in both the numerator and denominator.

2.2.8 Engineering Application

Engineering Application

Engineers frequently use formulae containing variables.

One of the most important examples is Newton’s Second Law:

\[ F=ma \]

where \(F\) is force, \(m\) is mass and \(a\) is acceleration.

If \(m=750\text{ kg}\) and \(a=1.8\text{ m/s}^2\), then

\[ F=(750)(1.8)=1350\text{ N} \]

Substitution allows engineers to use general equations to solve specific engineering problems.

2.2.9 Common Mistakes

Common errors include:

  • Forgetting to expand brackets.

  • Changing signs incorrectly when removing brackets.

  • Forgetting brackets when substituting negative numbers.

  • Ignoring BIDMAS after substitution.

  • Assuming \(2x^2=(2x)^2\).

  • Cancelling terms rather than factors in fractions.
